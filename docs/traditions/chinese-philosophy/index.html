Chinese Philosophy

◔ sketch  ·  anchor  ·  layer 1  ·  pass 2026-08-26.3

The classical period — the "hundred schools" of the Warring States centuries, roughly contemporaneous with classical Greece and the Buddha's India — is one of philosophy's great independent flowerings. Its organizing question was not "what is there?" but "what is the Way (dao)?": how should human life and political order be arranged, and what kind of person can arrange it? That the center of gravity is practical is not a deficit of theory. It determined which theoretical questions got asked — about human nature, language, action, and the sources of normativity — and they were asked with full rigor.

Kongzi (Confucius) set the agenda: the cultivation of humaneness (ren) through ritual propriety (li) — not empty formalism but the learned medium in which virtue is expressed, as grammar is the medium of speech — and the doctrine of rectifying names (zhengming): social titles carry normative content, and a ruler who does not rule as a ruler should is misnamed. The kinship with Greek virtue ethics is real, but the school of virtue here is the family and the rite, not the polis or the rational soul. Mozi built the first systematic counter-position: doctrines are to be tested by their benefit to the state and people, and by that test partiality fails — we owe impartial care (jian'ai) to all, and elaborate ritual, music, and funerals are waste. The resemblance to consequentialism is genuine, though the standard is public order and welfare, not aggregate utility.

Within Confucianism, Mengzi and Xunzi conducted a genuinely naturalistic debate about moral development. Mengzi argued that human nature is good: anyone glimpsing a child about to fall into a well feels alarm, and such "sprouts" of virtue need only cultivation, as a seedling needs tending. Xunzi answered that our nature is crude and disorderly; goodness is artifice — the achievement of ritual and deliberate reformation, as straight wood comes from the steaming-frame. The dispute turns on observation, thought experiment, and rival models of cultivation, not scripture.

The Daoist texts attack the whole project of ordering by names. The Daodejing attributed to Laozi opens by denying that the constant dao can be spoken, and commends wu-wei — non-coercive, uncontrived action — and the power of the low and yielding. Zhuangzi radicalizes this into a perspectivism about distinction-drawing itself: every judgment of "this/not-this" (shi/fei) issues from a standpoint with no neutral arbiter above it, and his skill stories (the ox-carving cook) suggest a knowing-how that outruns anything sayable. This is a sustained interrogation of the limits of language and disputation, with no Western original it copies.

Disputation nonetheless had its specialists. The School of Names (Hui Shi's paradoxes; Gongsun Long's "a white horse is not a horse") and the later Mohist Canons developed real logic and semantics — criteria for assertion, the behavior of names for kinds and stuffs, analyses of inference and paradox — a line cut short when Han Feizi's Legalism, the theory that order rests not on virtue but on impartial law, administrative technique, and positional power, armed the Qin unification.

The Han made Confucianism state orthodoxy inside a syncretic correlative cosmology; Buddhism's arrival then transformed the terrain for a millennium. The Song–Ming Neo-Confucian revival was metaphysics and moral psychology in its own right, not commentary: Zhu Xi's architecture of li (pattern, principle) and qi (vital stuff), with moral failure as qi obscuring pattern, and Wang Yangming's unity of knowledge and action — to know the good and not act is not yet to know it — grounded in innate moral knowing.

Twentieth-century New Confucianism (Xiong Shili, Mou Zongsan reading Kant) rebuilt the tradition in dialogue with European philosophy — under the shadow of Hegel's dismissal and the recurring question "is Chinese philosophy philosophy?" The instructive move is to turn the question around: what conception of philosophy — argument only in the treatise genre, problems only as one lineage carved them — makes it seem sensible? The aphorism, dialogue, and commentary carry argument differently, not less. Who counts as a philosopher, and by whose criteria, is the same live question the Indian reception faces.

Bridges

The densest bridges run into ethics — classical Confucian cultivation as an independent development of virtue ethics, Mohism as an independent consequentialism — and into politics, where the Confucian–Legalist dispute over virtue versus law as the basis of order is a foundational debate conducted with living stakes. The School of Names and the Mohist Canons bridge logic.history; Zhuangzi's perspectivism and the Daodejing's opening bridge language. Neo-Confucian moral psychology — pattern, mind, and Wang Yangming's knowledge-action unity — bridges mind. Within the register, buddhist-philosophy is the standing interlocutor for everything after the Han.
