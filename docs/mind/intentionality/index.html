Intentionality and Mental Content

◔ sketch  ·  anchor  ·  layer 1  ·  pass 2026-08-26.8

A belief about Vienna is about Vienna; a fear of ghosts is about ghosts, though there are none. No rock, river, or planet is about anything. Brentano proposed that this directedness — intentionality, from the scholastics' intentio — is the mark of the mental: every mental state points at an object, and the object need not exist. That last clause is the sting. Aboutness cannot be an ordinary relation, since relations require both relata; and it must somehow attach to states of a brain, which physics describes without ever mentioning what anything means. The problem of intentionality is to say how a piece of the physical world gets to have conditions of truth and satisfaction — to be right or wrong about something beyond itself.

The naturalization programs of the 1980s tried to answer with physical materials. Informational semantics (Dretske) starts from tracking: smoke carries information about fire, and a neural state that reliably covaries with cows could thereby mean cow. Its strength is that tracking is genuinely, measurably natural. Its signature failure is misrepresentation: a state sometimes triggered by horses on dark nights would then track cow-or-horse, and no error has occurred — the disjunction problem. Since a theory of content that cannot make room for false belief has changed the subject, the sophistications are attempts to buy error. Fodor's asymmetric dependence buys it with counterfactual structure: horses cause cow-tokens only because cows do, and not vice versa — an elegantly minimal fix, though critics ask what grounds the asymmetry other than the content it was meant to explain. Teleosemantics (Millikan, Papineau) buys it with biological function: a state means what it was selected to indicate, and misrepresents when it fires off-function, as the frog's snap at a BB does. Its strength is that biology already contains a kind of normativity — hearts can malfunction — for free. Its signature cost is Swampman: a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of you, congealed by chance in a swamp, has no selection history and hence, on the theory, no thoughts. Teleosemanticists mostly bite the bullet; the intuition that content is fixed by present constitution, not ancestry, does not go quietly.

A second axis crosses the first. Putnam's Twin Earth, built for linguistic meaning, was carried into the mind by Burge: your duplicate on a world where the clear drinkable liquid is XYZ has beliefs about XYZ, not water — so thought contents are partly fixed by environment and social practice. Externalism is now orthodox, but a rearguard defends narrow content, arguing that what explains behavior and is known from the inside must be shared by the twins. The phenomenal intentionality program is the internalist revival at full strength: intentionality is grounded in consciousness — there is an aboutness intrinsic to experience itself — and tracking-based content is derivative or ersatz. This reverses the naturalizers' order of explanation: instead of solving consciousness by first solving content, content waits on consciousness (bridge: mind.consciousness). Looming over all parties is the normativity of content: meaning something is being correctly applicable to some things and not others, and Kripke's Wittgenstein argues that no natural fact seems able to ground such a norm (bridge: language.rule-following).
