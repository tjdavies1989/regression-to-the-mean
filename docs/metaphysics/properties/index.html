Properties and Universals

◔ sketch  ·  anchor  ·  layer 1  ·  pass 2026-08-26.3

Two apples are both red. That is about as safe as a claim gets — and it already contains the problem. If both are red, they seem to have something in common; but what kind of thing could be wholly in one apple and wholly in another at once? Say instead that nothing is shared, and the sameness goes unexplained: calling both "red" starts to look like a coincidence of labeling. This is the problem of the One over Many, and every theory of properties chooses a horn.

Realists take the sharing literally: there is one entity, redness, a universal, multiply located in its instances. The strongest modern version is Armstrong's sparse, scientifically disciplined realism: not every predicate names a universal — none for being a game or being grue — and which universals exist is an empirical question, answered by total science rather than read off grammar. Universals earn their keep by grounding objective resemblance and, in Armstrong's hands, laws of nature. The standing wound is instantiation: if a particular's having a universal is a further relation, that relation must itself be instantiated, and Bradley's regress opens. Armstrong's reply: the fundamental units are states of affairs — a-being-F as a non-mereological unity, instantiation no added ingredient but the unity itself.

Trope theorists split the difference: properties are real but particular. This apple's redness and that apple's are two exactly resembling tropes, and objects may be bundles of them. The view keeps properties causally local — it is this redness that affects your eye — and avoids multiply located entities, at the price of taking exact resemblance among tropes as primitive.

Nominalists refuse the extra ontology altogether. Class nominalism identifies a property with the class of its instances, and stumbles on coextensive properties: creatures with hearts and creatures with kidneys form one class but seem two properties. Resemblance nominalism, revived with real rigor by Rodriguez-Pereyra, takes resemblance among particulars as primitive and builds property-classes from it, laboring against Goodman's puzzles of imperfect community and companionship. Quinean "ostrich" nominalism — Armstrong's insult, worn since as a badge — simply denies the demand: "a is red" is true, a exists, and no further analysis is owed; predicates need not name anything. Its strength is methodological hygiene; its cost is silence about why resemblance carves where it does.

Any adequate theory must also capture determinable–determinate structure: scarlet is a way of being red, and nothing scarlet is also crimson — an ordering, with exclusion built in, that neither bare universals nor bare classes obviously deliver.

The load-bearing compromise is Lewis's doctrine of natural properties: properties are simply classes (any class of possibilia is a property), but some classes are elite — perfectly natural — carving reality at its joints. Naturalness does a universal's work without a universal's ontology: it defines duplication and hence intrinsicality, supplies the vocabulary for the best-system account of laws, and, as reference magnetism, explains why our predicates mean green rather than grue. Lewis stayed neutral on whether naturalness is primitive or grounded in universals or tropes; either way, nearly every corner of his system — and much of the field since — leans on it. Where the old debate asked whether universals exist, the current one asks what naturalness is and how much may be charged to that account.

Bridges

  • To science: laws are the pressure point — Armstrong's laws are relations among universals, Lewis's best system needs natural properties for its vocabulary, and dispositional essentialism makes laws flow from the properties themselves.
  • To language: the One over Many begins as an inference from predication, ostrich nominalism blocks exactly that inference, and reference magnetism makes naturalness a semantic workhorse.
  • To metaphysics.modality: duplication, intrinsicality, and Lewis's abundant properties are defined across possibilia.
  • To metaphysics.grounding: the sparse–abundant distinction is a fundamentality claim; truthmaking for predications is a standing test case.
