Modality

◑ survey  ·  anchor  ·  layer 1  ·  pass 2026-08-26.7

Some truths could have been otherwise; others could not. There are eight planets, but there might have been nine; two and two make four, and nothing could have made them five. The distinction is as ordinary as regret and as entrenched as science, whose laws are supposed to support counterfactuals mere accidents do not. Yet everything we ever observe is actual. Where, then, is the difference between what is and what must be? The formal grammar of the question — boxes and diamonds, worlds and accessibility — is homed at logic.modal; the semantics runs identically on every answer to the ontological question, which is why that question falls here, and why this node now divides its work among four studies: what worlds are, what essence is, how modal truth is known, and how one thing shows up in many possibilities.

What worlds are is the field's centerpiece and its scandal. Lewis's modal realism makes the offer at full price — countless concrete universes, actuality as an indexical, plenitude by recombination — and what the price buys is the only genuine reduction on the market: counterfactuals, properties, propositions, and modality itself analyzed with no modal residue. The structural objections (island universes ruled out by the isolation criterion's double duty, recombination's cardinality paradoxes, Adams's ethical objection) and the priced-in incredulous stare have not produced a successful rival so much as a market of partial retreats: linguistic ersatzism smuggling "consistent" back in and blind to alien possibilities, magical ersatzism buying actualism with an ineffable primitive, Stalnaker's cheerful abandonment of the reductive demand, and modal fictionalism impaled on Brock–Rosen. The standing joke carries the field's real question: almost nobody believes Lewis and almost everybody uses him — theft over honest toil, or proof the machinery never needed the metaphysics. The full accounting lives at worlds.

Essence was rehabilitated twice. Kripke made de re necessity respectable against Quine's mockery — necessity of origin, kind, and composition, argued rather than asserted — and uncoupled the necessary from the a priori in both directions, leaving the necessary a posteriori for two-dimensionalism to domesticate. Fine then inverted the received order: essence cannot be defined as necessary property-having (Socrates necessarily belongs to {Socrates}, yet nothing in what Socrates is involves that set); essence is real definition, and necessity flows from it. The essence-first program and its conventionalist and normativist deflations — modal truths as object-language shadows of semantic rules — contest whether essence is discovered or legislated, at essence.

Modal epistemology carries more philosophical weight than any epistemology so unsettled should bear. Conceivability principles, refined through Yablo and Chalmers into ideal positive conceivability with the primary/secondary distinction absorbing Kripkean counterexamples, still deliver philosophy's biggest conclusions — zombies, disembodiment, ontological arguments — from philosophy's shakiest premises; van Inwagen's modal skepticism about remote possibility claims bites exactly there. Williamson's deflation grounds modal knowledge in ordinary counterfactual competence, vindicating the kitchen-table cases while leaving the exotic ones exactly as doubtful as they deserve to be; essence-first and empiricist alternatives compete at modal-epistemology.

Counterpart theory asks how one thing shows up in many possibilities: strictly transworld individuals, or world-bound things represented elsewhere by their closest counterparts? Kripke deflated the classical "problem" of transworld identity — worlds are stipulated, not spied through telescopes — and pressed the Humphrey objection against Lewis's counterparts; the theory's real selling point is the inconstancy of de re modality, letting the statue and the clay differ modally without coincident-object extravagance, with haecceitism and Chisholm's paradox as the deep water. See counterparts.

Across all four runs the hyperintensional frontier: essence, ground, and content cut finer than necessary equivalence, so worlds — whatever they are — may be too coarse a sieve for the distinctions metaphysics now trades in. Whether the future belongs to impossible worlds, structured contents, or truthmaker semantics is a question this node shares with logic.modal and metaphysics.grounding.
