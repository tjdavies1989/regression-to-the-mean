Metaphysics

◑ survey  ·  anchor  ·  layer 0  ·  pass 2026-08-26.3

Metaphysics asks what there is and what it is like, at the level of generality where the question survives abstraction from any particular subject matter. Every science assumes that things exist, persist, cause, and could have been otherwise; metaphysics is where those assumptions stop being assumed. No division of philosophy has been declared dead more often — Hume consigned it to the flames, Kant confined it to the bounds of possible experience, the logical empiricists diagnosed it as unmeaning noise — and none has revived more completely: the Kripkean rehabilitation of necessity and the Lewisian demonstration that systematic metaphysics could be done with analytic rigor made it, by century's end, the analytic tradition's flagship. The revival brought the old accusation indoors, where it now lives as a subfield: whether ontological disputes are substantive or verbal is itself one of the division's ten problems.

Being. The theory of existence begins from the twentieth century's great settlement — Quine's doctrine that to be is to be the value of a bound variable, with ontological commitment read off regimented best theory — and from its steady unraveling: Meinongian objects that have properties without having being, existence as a first-order property after all, an abstract/concrete divide the settlement presupposed but never explained, and the revived project of categories of being. Beneath the unraveling lies the founding quarrel between Carnap's internal/external distinction and Quine's collapse of it, and that quarrel's modern rerun is housed with grounding and fundamentality: the in-virtue-of relation that modal notions provably cannot draw (Socrates's singleton exists because Socrates does), priority monism against pluralism, and the metametaphysical contest — quantifier variance, easy ontology, joint-carving structure — over whether any of these disputes is about the world or about words. Grounding is at once the division's newest primitive and its conscience; its skeptics ask whether "big-G" grounding does any work its instances were not already doing.

Sameness. Identity looks trivial — everything is what it is — until Leibniz's Law meets change: the candle straight at noon and bent at midnight, Lois's divided beliefs, the statue and its clay. The theories of persistence (endurantism, perdurantism, stage theory) are rival solutions to the problem of temporary intrinsics, stress-tested by the ship of Theseus and by Evans's one-page argument against vague identity; Kripke's proof of the necessity of identity ties the topic to the modal machinery next door. The material objects node takes the mereological sequel: van Inwagen's special composition question, the vagueness argument for universalism, coincident objects and the grounding problem for constitution, and the standing quarrel between revisionary metaphysics and the defense of ordinary things.

Necessity. The metaphysics of modality owns the questions the formalism at logic.modal prices but cannot pay: what possible worlds are — Lewis's concrete plurality, whose explanatory yield no rival has matched and whose ontology almost no one accepts, against the ersatz constructions that surrender the reduction to keep the actuality — and which properties things have essentially. Fine's reversal (essence as real definition, with necessity flowing from it rather than defining it) reshaped the field and helped ignite the grounding literature; Kripke's necessary a posteriori uncoupled metaphysical from epistemic modality and left modal epistemology — conceivability against counterfactual competence — carrying more philosophical weight than any epistemology so unsettled should have to bear.

Connection. Causation is how the world hangs together, and its modern literature is the working-out of Hume's two non-equivalent "definitions": the regularity program through Mackie's INUS conditions, the counterfactual program through Lewis and the preemption epicycles that consumed it, probabilistic and process theories, interventionism as the current lingua franca of science and statistics — analysis or mere methodology being the open question — and the anti-Humean revival of causal powers. Time and space inherit the other half of the world's structure: McTaggart's argument whose wreckage maps the field, the presentism/eternalism/growing-block contest under direct pressure from relativity's frame-relative simultaneity, passage against projection, the thermodynamic arrow, substantivalism against relationism from Newton's bucket to the hole argument, and time travel made respectable jointly by Lewis's compatibilism about the grandfather paradox and Gödel's rotating universes.

Persons. Two problems are homed here because their metaphysics outruns their psychology and their ethics. Free will is organized by the consequence argument — if determinism is true, our acts are consequences of laws and past we never controlled — and by the ensuing map: compatibilism from conditional analyses through Frankfurt cases to reasons-responsiveness and Strawson's reactive attitudes, libertarianism event-causal and agent-causal, hard incompatibilism and the manipulation arguments, with the normative status of responsibility split off to ethics and the empirical will to philosophy of mind. Personal identity runs from Locke's memory criterion through Parfit's fission cases to the deflationary conclusion that identity is not what matters in survival — against animalism's insistence that we were never psychological entities to begin with, and alongside the no-self analyses, Humean and Buddhist, that the traditions register treats on their own terms.

Properties underlie all of it, and the One over Many — what two red apples share — turns out to be the division's quiet load-bearer: Armstrong's sparse universals grounding resemblance and law, tropes keeping properties particular, the nominalisms refusing the ontology, and Lewis's natural properties — elite classes carving at the joints — doing a universal's work without a universal's ontology in the theories of laws, duplication, intrinsicality, and reference that half this corpus relies on.

The division's figures register is dense: aristotle drew its table of contents, ibn-sina gave existence its first great analysis, hume and kant set its limits, lewis rebuilt its systematic ambitions. Its busiest borders are with logic (modality, identity, vagueness), mind (persons, causation's mental instance), and science (laws, spacetime, naturalized metametaphysics).

Bridges

  • To logic: modality's formalism (logic.modal), vague identity and the sorites (logic.paradox.vagueness), and ontological commitment's first-order regimentation.
  • To mind: free will and personal identity do half their work there; the mind–body problem is a metaphysical dispute conducted on mind's terrain; mental causation is causation's hardest instance.
  • To science: laws, spacetime, and the direction of time are joint property with philosophy of physics; naturalized metametaphysics asks whether science should replace the armchair.
  • To language: rigid designation and the necessary a posteriori show semantic argument settling modal questions; truth and truthmaking keep the border open.
  • To ethics and religion: moral realism is an ontological thesis; classical theism stakes claims about necessary being, causation, and time — carried by aristotle, ibn-sina, aquinas, hume, lewis in the figures register.
