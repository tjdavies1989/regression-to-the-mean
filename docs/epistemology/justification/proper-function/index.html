Proper Functionalism and Warrant

◔ sketch  ·  satellite  ·  layer 2  ·  pass 2026-09-09.22

Plantinga's two 1993 volumes, Warrant: The Current Debate and Warrant and Proper Function, begin by changing the subject. "Justification," he argues, carries a deontological inheritance from Descartes and Locke — having done one's epistemic duty — and duty-fulfillment is compatible with every cognitive disaster. What the analysis of knowledge needs is warrant: whatever, added to true belief, yields knowledge. The first volume is a catalogue of counterexamples. Internalism fails because a subject can be blameless while a brain lesion feeds her beliefs; coherentism fails for the Epistemically Inflexible Climber, coherent as the world changes around a frozen mind. The decisive case is against reliabilism: a lesion that reliably causes its victim to believe he has a brain lesion yields true belief by a reliable process, and nobody credits him with knowledge. What the lesion cases share is malfunction.

The positive account has four conditions. A belief has warrant when produced by cognitive faculties (i) functioning properly — free of malfunction — (ii) in an environment sufficiently like the one for which they were designed, (iii) according to a segment of the design plan aimed at truth rather than survival, comfort, or loyalty, and (iv) where the design plan is good: there is a high objective probability that beliefs so produced are true. Degree of warrant tracks firmness of belief. "Design plan" is officially neutral between a designer and a specification of how a thing works when it works well, though Plantinga argues that naturalism can give the notion no content — the seed of his evolutionary argument against naturalism.

The Gettier claim. Plantinga's diagnosis of Gettier cases is that in each the environment side fails: faculties work properly, but the local circumstances — fake barns, the stopped clock, the borrowed Ford — are ones the design plan was not built for. Warrant is then anti-luck by construction, and Plantinga endorsed Merricks's thesis (1995) that warrant, in the degree required for knowledge, entails truth. The claim was quickly tested. Feldman, Greco, and Sosa produced cases where faculties work properly in a congenial environment and still deliver luckily true belief; Zagzebski (1994) argued that any account leaving a gap between warrant and truth admits Gettier cases as a matter of form. Plantinga's reply ("Warrant and Accidentally True Belief," 1997) added the resolution condition: the belief must be formed in a mini-environment favorable to the faculty's exercise. Critics read the addition as conceding the game: the condition names the absence of luck rather than explaining it.

Swampman. A molecule-for-molecule duplicate congealed by lightning has no history and no designer, hence no design plan, hence — on the theory — no warrant, though he seems to see, remember, and reason exactly as his original does. Sosa (1993) used the case to argue that proper functionalism is virtue reliabilism in a mask: what does the work is reliability in a design-type environment, and once that is said, "design" drops out. Plantinga bites: Swampman has no proper function, and the intuition that he knows comes from his resembling things that do. Boyce and Moon (2016) argue the intuition can be debunked rather than merely denied. The more constructive reply is Graham's (2012, 2014), on which functions are etiological in the biological sense — acquired through selection, but also through feedback in learning — so a Swampman who lives long enough acquires them, and the case shrinks to a dispute about his first minutes.

Naturalized proper functionalism. Bergmann's Justification without Awareness (2006) returns the theory to justification and removes the theology: a belief is justified when produced by faculties functioning properly, in the kind of environment they were designed for, and not subject to defeat — "design" read through the etiological theory of function. The dividend is the new evil demon: the victim's faculties function properly in an environment they were not designed for, so she is justified without being reliable, an answer reliabilism cannot give. The cost is teleosemantics' own — justification now depends on a history the believer cannot inspect.

The religious application. The account was built for a use. If theism is true, humans possess a truth-aimed faculty — the sensus divinitatis of the Aquinas/Calvin model — and belief in God it produces has warrant; if theism is false, it almost certainly does not. The de jure question, whether theistic belief is rational, therefore cannot be settled apart from the de facto question whether it is true. Critics reply that this is precisely the difficulty: warrant is hostage to a metaphysics, and every party can run the argument in its own favor. The debate is homed at religion.faith-reason.

Children

Thin node — no natural children yet. Reformed epistemology, the account's principal application, is stubbed under religion.faith-reason.

Bridges

  • religion.faith-reason: reformed epistemology, proper basicality, and the Great Pumpkin lineage are homed there; this node supplies the theory of warrant they run on.
  • epistemology.knowledge: warrant is the Gettier quantity by definition; Merricks's warrant-entails-truth thesis and Zagzebski's inescapability argument are that node's business too.
  • epistemology.justification.reliabilism and epistemology.justification.internalism-externalism: Sosa's reduction of proper function to reliability-in-a-design-environment, and Bergmann's use of proper function against the new evil demon, are fought across these three nodes.
  • science.biology and mind.intentionality: the etiological theory of function and the Swampman case are shared debts; Graham's naturalization borrows both.
