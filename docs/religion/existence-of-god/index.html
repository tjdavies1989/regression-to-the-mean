Arguments for the Existence of God

◔ sketch  ·  anchor  ·  layer 1  ·  pass 2026-08-26.13

Natural theology wagers that unaided reason can establish, or at least confirm, that God exists. The wager is still live: arguments dismissed in one generation's textbooks return sharpened in the next. What follows are the families at their strongest.

The cosmological family argues from the world's existence to its ground, and it is really several arguments. The Kalām argument, revived by Craig, is temporal: whatever begins to exist has a cause; the universe began to exist; so it has a cause — one that, being outside space and time, looks unsettlingly like a person with a will. Its distinctive support is the case against actual infinities (Hilbert's Hotel and its paradoxes), buttressed by Big Bang cosmology; its critics ask whether causal principles calibrated inside the universe apply to the universe, and whether cosmology really delivers an absolute beginning. Leibnizian contingency arguments drop time altogether: the totality of contingent things demands an explanation that no contingent thing can supply, so there is a necessary being. Here everything turns on the Principle of Sufficient Reason — the Hume–Edwards reply that explaining each member explains the whole, the brute-fact option, and van Inwagen's argument that an unrestricted PSR collapses contingency itself. Aquinas's Five Ways, read as metaphysics rather than museum pieces, are a third thing again: not a first cause long ago but a sustaining cause now, terminating an essentially ordered series in something whose essence just is its existence. The purest version predates all of these: Ibn Sīnā's proof that whatever exists is in itself either necessary or contingent, and that the sum of contingents, taken as one, must be tipped into existence from outside — by the Necessary Existent, whose uniqueness and simplicity he then derives.

The ontological family tries to close the question from the armchair. Anselm: that than which nothing greater can be conceived cannot exist in the understanding alone. Descartes: existence belongs to the essence of a supremely perfect being as its angle-sum belongs to a triangle. Kant's reply — existence is not a real predicate, adding nothing to a concept — was refined by the quantifier analysis and looked decisive until the modal versions arrived. Plantinga's: if maximal greatness is so much as possible, then by S5 it is actual. Plantinga concedes the argument proves nothing to someone who doubts the possibility premise — its yield is the rationality of belief, not a demonstration — while Gödel's formalization, deriving necessary existence from axioms about "positive properties," faces the charge of modal collapse.

The design family argues from order. Paley's artifact analogy was pre-emptively dismantled in Hume's Dialogues — the analogy is thin, the evidence underdetermines one perfect designer, order might be the residue of blind trials — and Darwin supplied the mechanism its biological version lacked. The argument returned at the level of physics: the constants of nature appear fine-tuned for life, and the serious responses are the multiverse with anthropic selection and disputes over whether our observation of a life-permitting universe is evidence at all (the firing-squad rejoinder says yes).

Moral arguments contend that objective obligation or moral knowledge is best explained theistically; pragmatic arguments are homed under faith and reason. Finally, Swinburne reframes the enterprise: no single argument is a proof, but theism is a simple large-scale hypothesis and each argument a piece of Bayesian evidence in a cumulative case — a methodology whose contested core is the prior probability of God.

Bridges

  • To metaphysics.modality: the modal ontological argument runs on S5 and a possibility premise; necessary existence is a modal thesis before it is a theological one.
  • To metaphysics.causation: every cosmological argument stakes a causal or explanatory principle whose scope is exactly what the critic contests.
  • To epistemology.formal: Swinburne's cumulative case is applied Bayesian confirmation theory, priors and all.
  • To figures: Ibn Sīnā's necessary-existent proof, Hume's Dialogues, Kant's critique of the proofs, Descartes's Fifth Meditation, and Gödel's formalization each anchor a family here.
