# Metaphysics

Metaphysics asks what there is, and what the things there are must be like, at a level of generality beneath any particular science. Physics tells us there are electrons; metaphysics asks what it takes for *anything* to exist, and whether numbers, possibilities, and absences exist in the same sense electrons do. The questions are at once unavoidable and slippery: everyone operates with answers to them, yet once made explicit, the ordinary picture starts to creak.

Consider three of the creaks. First, sameness over time. A ship whose planks are replaced one by one seems to survive each replacement; yet if the old planks are reassembled there are two candidates for being the original ship, and at most one can be it. Endurantists hold that objects are wholly present at each moment; perdurantists reply that they are four-dimensional wholes of temporal parts. Second, necessity. It seems *impossible* that two and two make five, and merely contingent that you are reading this sentence — but what grounds the difference? Lewis took possible worlds to be concrete universes as real as our own; actualists build them from actual properties and propositions. Third, resemblance. Two apples are both red. Realists say they literally share one entity, redness, wholly present in each; nominalists say there are only particulars resembling one another, and that universals explain nothing the resemblance did not already give us.

The pattern — an everyday certainty, a puzzle that fractures it, rival systematic repairs — recurs across the division. Causation seems the cement of the universe, yet Hume's challenge stands: observe as closely as you like, you find succession and regularity, never the pushing itself. Time seems to flow, yet the "block universe" reading of relativity treats past, present, and future as equally real. Choices seem free, yet each is either determined or not, and randomness looks no more like freedom than determinism does — hence the compatibilist wager that freedom never required determinism's falsity.

The field articulates along these problems: existence and ontology; identity and persistence; modality; causation; time and space; properties; the metaphysics of persons (free will and personal identity are homed here); the mereology of material things; and grounding — which facts hold *in virtue of* which — together with metametaphysical doubts about whether ontological disputes are substantive at all. Its boundaries are porous: metaphysics of mind shades into philosophy of mind, laws and spacetime into philosophy of science, modality into logic and language. The working criterion is generality — metaphysics keeps the questions that survive abstraction from any particular subject matter.

## Bridges

- To `mind`: free will and personal identity are homed here but do half their work there; the mind–body problem is itself a metaphysical dispute.
- To `science`: the metaphysics of time, causation, and laws is continuous with philosophy of physics; naturalized metametaphysics asks whether science should replace armchair ontology.
- To `logic` and `language`: modal logic disciplines modality; the Quinean method reads ontological commitment off regimented language.
- To `ethics` and `religion`: moral realism is an ontological thesis, and classical theism stakes claims about necessary being, causation, and time — routes carried by figures from Aristotle and Avicenna to Hume and Lewis.
