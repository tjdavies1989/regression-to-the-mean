# Existence and Ontology

Everyone can list some of what exists: tables, electrons, other people. But push the list outward and the confidence drains. Numbers? Holes? Fictional detectives? The average taxpayer? Each is something we talk about, quantify over, even count — and each sits awkwardly in a world of concrete things. Ontology asks two questions that turn out to be entangled: *what exists*, and *what existence is*. The second is the deeper one, because every answer to the first presupposes a standard for settling it.

The twentieth century's dominant answer was Quine's, and it was a settlement in both senses: a doctrine and a truce. To be is to be the value of a bound variable. Existence is not a property some things have and others lack — there are no others — but what the existential quantifier expresses. Ontological questions are therefore settled indirectly: regiment your best total theory of the world into first-order logic, and you are committed to whatever the theory's bound variables must range over for it to be true. The strength of the settlement is its discipline. "Exists" means one thing; there are no degrees or modes of being; disputes about numbers or universals become tractable questions about whether our best theory can be paraphrased to avoid quantifying over them. Ontology became a research program with a method.

The settlement has been unraveling on several fronts. Meinongians deny the equation of quantification with existence: we can think about, fear, and truly describe things that do not exist — Pegasus has wings, the fountain of youth was sought by Ponce de León — so some objects have properties without having being. Dismissed for decades on Russell's and Quine's authority, the view has serious modern defenders (Parsons, Zalta, Priest), and its strongest card is the data of intentionality, which Quinean paraphrase handles awkwardly at best. A related revolt holds that existence is a first-order property of individuals after all — one that everything happens to have, or that Meinongian objects lack — against the Frege–Russell doctrine that it is a second-order property of concepts. Meanwhile the abstract/concrete divide, which the settlement presupposed rather than explained, resists analysis: no proposed criterion — causal inertness, non-spatiotemporality — cleanly sorts the cases. And the older project of a system of *categories of being*, from Aristotle's substances and accidents to Lowe's four-category ontology, has returned, insisting that a flat domain of "values of variables" erases structural distinctions ontology exists to draw.

Beneath all this lies the founding quarrel. Carnap distinguished questions internal to a linguistic framework ("are there prime numbers over a hundred?" — trivially yes, within the number framework) from external questions about whether to adopt the framework, which he took to be pragmatic, not factual. Quine, having rejected the analytic/synthetic distinction on which the contrast rested, collapsed the two: there is just one question of what to include in best theory. Contemporary metametaphysics — quantifier variance, easy ontology, ontological realism — is this dispute rerun with better tools; it is homed at `metaphysics.grounding` and only flagged here. One boundary note: mathematical objects, ontology's most storied test case, are homed at `logic.mathematics.ontology`; this node owns the general machinery that debate deploys.

## Children

- `existence` — **Existence** — whether existence is a first-order property of individuals or a second-order property of concepts, with free logic as the formal arena. *(standard)*
- `commitment` — **Ontological Commitment** — Quine's criterion, its paraphrase discipline, and rivals that sever quantification from commitment. *(standard)*
- `nonexistents` — **Nonexistent Objects** — Meinong's theory, Russell's objections, and the modern reconstructions of Parsons, Zalta, and Priest. *(standard)*
- `abstract-objects` — **Abstract Objects** — the abstract/concrete divide, the way of abstraction, and whether abstracta earn their keep outside mathematics. *(anchor)*
- `categories` — **Categories of Being** — systems of ontological categories from Aristotle to Lowe, and whether being divides into kinds at all. *(standard)*
- `fictional-objects` — **Fictional Objects** — the ontology of characters and creatures of fiction, from Meinongian to artifactual theories. *(satellite)*
