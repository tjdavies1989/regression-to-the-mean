# Free Will

Determinism is the thesis that a complete description of the world at any one time, together with the laws of nature, entails everything that ever happens afterward. Van Inwagen's consequence argument turns this into a threat: if determinism is true, your acts are consequences of the laws and of the state of the world long before your birth. The remote past is not up to you; the laws are not up to you; and whatever follows by necessity from things not up to you is, plausibly, not up to you either. So if determinism is true, nothing you do is up to you. The argument's pull comes from the closure of each escape route: deny the past's fixity and you claim power over history; deny the laws' fixity and you claim power to break physics. Nor does indeterminism obviously rescue anything — an act that merely *happens*, undetermined, looks like chance, and chance seems no more yours than necessity. This is the real problem: freedom appears to require that determinism be false, yet its falsity appears to give us randomness rather than agency.

Compatibilists deny the first appearance. The classical conditional analysis (Hobbes, Hume, Moore) read "she could have done otherwise" as "she would have done otherwise, had she chosen to" — a claim determinism leaves intact. The analysis stumbled over compulsion, but Frankfurt's 1969 cases opened a deeper move: imagine a counterfactual intervener who would have forced your act had you wavered, but never intervenes. You act on your own, seemingly responsible, yet could not have done otherwise. If that verdict stands, the principle of alternate possibilities is false, and freedom lives in the *actual* sequence of the act, not in forking paths. Fischer and Ravizza built the leading actual-sequence account: an agent acts freely when the mechanism issuing her act is moderately reasons-responsive and is her own. Strawson's "Freedom and Resentment" reframes the whole dispute: resentment, gratitude, and indignation constitute our interpersonal life, and the practice they compose neither needs nor could receive a metaphysical guarantee.

Libertarians accept incompatibilism and bet against determinism. Kane's event-causal account locates indeterminism in "torn" decisions where a divided will makes either resolution the agent's own — but here the luck objection bites hardest: if everything about you left both outcomes open, the difference between them seems a matter of luck, not control. Agent-causal theories (Reid, Chisholm, O'Connor) answer luck by making the agent herself, a substance rather than an event, the cause of her decisions; the answer's strength is exactly measured by the exoticness of the posit and its uneasy fit with a scientific picture of causation.

Pereboom's hard incompatibilism closes the pincer: determinism precludes the freedom that grounds basic desert, and event-causal indeterminism does no better. His four-case manipulation argument challenges compatibilists to find a principled line between an agent covertly engineered by neuroscientists and one ordinarily determined; each case shades into the next. The skeptical conclusion has practical stakes — punishment justified by desert loses its license — though Pereboom argues that quarantine-style prevention, love, and meaning survive, and the debate over what a desert-free ethics could keep is now itself a literature.

## Children

- `consequence-argument` — **The Consequence Argument** — van Inwagen's formalizations, local-miracle and Humean compatibilist replies, and the fixity of past and law. *(standard)*
- `alternate-possibilities` — **Alternate Possibilities** — the principle that responsibility requires ability to do otherwise, Frankfurt cases, and flicker-of-freedom and dilemma defenses. *(standard)*
- `compatibilism` — **Compatibilism** — from conditional analyses through mesh, reasons-responsiveness, and Strawsonian accounts of freedom within determinism. *(anchor)*
- `libertarianism` — **Libertarianism** — event-causal, agent-causal, and non-causal accounts of undetermined free action, and the luck objection to each. *(anchor)*
- `skepticism` — **Free-Will Skepticism** — hard determinism, hard incompatibilism, manipulation arguments, and life without basic desert. *(standard)*
