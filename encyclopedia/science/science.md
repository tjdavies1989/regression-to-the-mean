# Philosophy of Science

Science is the best-behaved knowledge humanity produces — cumulative where philosophy circles, self-correcting where tradition entrenches — and for exactly that reason it is philosophy's favorite object: if we could say *why* science works, we would hold the theory of knowledge's crown jewel. The division's history is the repeated failure of clean answers to survive contact with real science, and the discipline that failure built. The logical empiricists' formal reconstructions (explanation as derivation, confirmation as a logical relation) broke on counterexamples; Kuhn's historical turn broke the image of steady accumulation; the practice turn broke the assumption that theories, rather than models, experiments, and instruments, are where science lives. The field now runs in two registers — general philosophy of science, and the philosophies of the special sciences, where physics, biology, and the social sciences generate problems no general account settles — and its perennial condition is captured by its own founding jokes: philosophers of science know that explanation is what science does, and cannot say what explanation is.

**Explanation and confirmation** are the old core. Hempel's covering-law model — to explain is to derive from laws — died of asymmetry (the flagpole explains the shadow's length, never conversely) and irrelevance, and its succession contest (Salmon's causal-mechanical account, Kitcher's unificationism, van Fraassen's pragmatics, the new mechanists) turned into the discovery that explanation may be plural: causal where it can be, mathematical and structural where it must be — the non-causal cases now pressing hardest. Confirmation inherited Hume's problem and added its own scandals — Hempel's ravens, Goodman's grue, which converted the old problem of induction into the new problem of which predicates project — and settled into Bayesianism as lingua franca, with the priors and old-evidence problems as standing debts and Mayo's severe testing as the frequentist opposition; Popper's falsificationism survives in scientific self-image long after its philosophical troubles were tallied.

**Laws of nature** hold the division's border with metaphysics: the Humean best-system analysis (laws as the theorems of the simplest, strongest summary of the mosaic) against governing conceptions (Armstrong's necessitation) against dispositional essentialism (laws flowing from what properties are) — with counterfactual support as the datum all must earn, ceteris paribus laws of the special sciences as everyone's embarrassment, and Cartwright's dappled-world heresy denying fundamental laws their imperial scope.

**The realism debate** is the division's centerpiece contest, driven by two engines each strongest where the other is weak: the no-miracles argument (success without truth would be a cosmic coincidence — and novel prediction is its best cartridge) owns the present; the pessimistic meta-induction (the graveyard of successful dead theories: phlogiston, caloric, the ether) owns the past, sharpened by Stanford's unconceived alternatives. Van Fraassen's constructive empiricism holds the antirealist line at observability and pays the arbitrary-boundary objection; the live positions retreat from wholesale verdicts to retail ones — structural realism (believe what survives theory change: the equations, not the natures; ontic in Ladyman's hands), selective realism crediting only working posits, Hacking's entity realism grounding belief in manipulation. The drift of the whole debate is from philosophy of science to philosophy of particular sciences and episodes.

**Models and idealization** are the practice turn's yield: science's daily instruments are deliberately false — frictionless planes, infinite populations, rational agents — and the puzzle is how the strictly false teaches truly. Models as mediators between theory and world, the fictions view, the representation debate (similarity, structure, inference), simulation's epistemology, and robustness analysis form the field where philosophy of science now most resembles science studies without surrendering normativity.

**Theory change** is Kuhn's estate, read carefully: paradigms and normal science, incommensurability in its semantic, methodological, and perceptual versions, and what *Structure* did and did not claim about rationality; Lakatos's research programmes as the rationalist repair, Laudan's problem-solving accountancy, Feyerabend's provocation, and the science wars as reception history. Whether revolutions are real or historiographic artifacts — the continuity thesis — remains open, and the debate's stakes flow directly into realism via the meta-induction.

**The special sciences.** Philosophy of physics confronts the measurement problem — Everett's branching worlds and their probability problem, Bohm's pilot waves, dynamical collapse — as the deepest interpretive question in science, with Bell's theorem policing every proposed escape; statistical mechanics holds the thermodynamic arrow and the Past Hypothesis; the ontology of spacetime is split by editorial decision with `metaphysics.time-space`. Philosophy of biology dismantles the physics template: selection's units and levels, the spandrels critique of adaptationism, function's two analyses (etiological and causal-role) as naturalized teleology's hinge, the species problem, biological individuality, and the gene's informational metaphor. The philosophy of the social sciences inherits the naturalism-interpretivism contest — laws or Verstehen — plus Hacking's looping kinds, whose objects answer back. **Values in science** closes the circle reflexively: the value-free ideal against inductive risk (accepting a hypothesis risks errors whose costs are moral), underdetermination as values' doorway, and the epistemic/non-epistemic distinction's instability — the division's own contribution to the politics of knowledge.

## Bridges

- **Epistemology**: confirmation is formal epistemology applied; the social structure of inquiry meets `epistemology.social`; naturalized epistemology runs the border both ways.
- **Metaphysics**: laws, causation, and time are joint property; the realism debate is ontology conducted on scientific evidence.
- **Logic**: probability theory and the mathematics the sciences deploy; `logic.mathematics.ontology.indispensability` leans on confirmational holism held here.
- **Mind**: cognitive science's foundations are homed at `mind.cognitive-science`; biology's function analyses feed teleosemantics.
- **Politics**: science advice, expertise, and the governance of inquiry meet political epistemology; values in science is the seam.
