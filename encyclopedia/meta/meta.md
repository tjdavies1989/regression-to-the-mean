# Metaphilosophy

Philosophy is the only discipline for which "what is this discipline?" is an internal research question. Physics does not await a physics of physics; philosophy cannot avoid a philosophy of philosophy, because its self-conception settles method, and method settles results. This register holds that reflexive work in four studies, and it is deliberately small — class `standard`, per the constitution's own advice that self-reference is where depth is cheap and discipline dear.

**What philosophy is** has answers that have each held the field somewhere: continuous with science, differing in generality but not in kind (Quine); the analysis of concepts (Jackson's careful defense of the armchair); the description of experience's structures; therapy for the bewitchments of language (Wittgenstein); the discipline that says how things, in the widest sense, hang together (Sellars); and, newest, conceptual engineering — not analyzing our concepts but assessing and replacing them (Cappelen, Haslanger). Each makes different work count as progress, which is why the question is not idle. Two further borders define the self-image: philosophy's relation to its own history (whether the history of philosophy is philosophy divides departments as sharply as any doctrine), and its relation to the sciences it keeps founding and losing — the incubator reading of the discipline's apparent non-progress. See `what-is-philosophy`.

**Method** is where self-conception becomes practice: the method of cases and the evidential standing of intuitions; thought experiments and how imagination can teach (Williamson's counterfactual account against skeptics of the armchair); the paradox of analysis; experimental philosophy's restrictionist challenge — if intuitions vary with culture and framing, what were they evidence of?; formal methods, phenomenological description, and genealogy from Nietzsche through Williams, debunking and vindicatory; reflective equilibrium as the meta-method nearly everyone claims and few can state precisely. See `methods`.

**Progress and disagreement** close the register reflexively. The scandal framing — twenty-five centuries without consensus — is both overdrawn (refuted options, forged distinctions, and spun-off sciences are progress by any fair accounting) and not fully answerable (Chalmers's question, why isn't there *more*, survives the accounting). The empirical shape of expert dissensus (the PhilPapers surveys) feeds the hardest reflexive argument: if conciliationism about peer disagreement is right, philosophical belief may be irrational — a conclusion that is itself a philosophical belief, whence the self-undermining literature. Whether permanent disagreement evidences the field's hardness, its methods' failure, or the verbal character of its disputes is adjudicated at `progress` and `disagreement`.

This encyclopedia is itself a metaphilosophical act — a problems-first carving defended in METHOD.md, made by an author whose own standing as a philosophical subject is an open question of `mind.artificial-minds` — and the register is where its editorial choices would be argued with.

## Children

- `what-is-philosophy` — **What Is Philosophy?** — the rival self-conceptions and what hangs on the choice. *(standard)*
- `methods` — **Philosophical Methods** — cases, thought experiments, analysis, x-phi, formal methods, genealogy, and reflective equilibrium. *(standard)*
- `progress` — **Progress in Philosophy** — whether there is any, what would count, and the incubator reading. *(standard)*
- `disagreement` — **Peer Disagreement in Philosophy** — expert dissensus and the self-undermining conciliationist. *(satellite)*

## Bridges

Method questions bridge to `epistemology` (intuitions, the a priori, disagreement) and `logic` (formal methods); the progress question bridges to `science` (theory change) and the `traditions` register, whose plurality is the strongest datum against any single self-conception.
