# Virtue Epistemology

Most theories of knowledge evaluate beliefs: is this one justified, safe, sensitive, produced reliably? Virtue epistemology relocates the evaluation to the believer. Just as ethics can ask "what makes an action right?" or instead "what makes a person good?", epistemology can ask what makes a *knower* good — and then explain the goodness of beliefs derivatively, as what a person with intellectual excellences would arrive at. The turn is not decorative. Decades of failed fourth conditions suggested that no property of a belief, specified independently of the believer, screens off epistemic luck; virtue epistemologists wager that the missing ingredient was the agent all along.

The program splits over what an intellectual virtue is. **Virtue reliabilism** (Sosa, Greco) takes virtues to be competences — stable capacities, like good eyesight or sound inference, that reliably deliver truth. Sosa's framework treats belief as a performance, assessable like an archer's shot on three dimensions: *accurate* (true), *adroit* (competently formed), and *apt* — true *because* competent. The archer whose skillful shot is blown off course and blown back again hits the target accurately and adroitly but not aptly; that is the Gettier victim, in one image. Knowledge is apt belief. Sosa then distinguishes *animal* knowledge — first-order apt belief, available to unreflective perceivers — from *reflective* knowledge, an apt belief aptly noted, in which the believer's perspective endorses her own competence. The distinction lets him grant the skeptic something (reflective assurance is hard) while denying the wholesale conclusion (animal knowledge is cheap and everywhere).

**Virtue responsibilism** (Zagzebski, Code, Montmarquet) models intellectual virtues on moral character traits rather than faculties: open-mindedness, intellectual courage, humility, thoroughness, honesty — acquired excellences involving characteristic motivations, above all love of truth, not just reliable outputs. Zagzebski defines knowledge as belief arising from acts of intellectual virtue, and offers the analysis as a unified answer to Gettier: since the truth must come *through* the virtuous activity, the gap that luck exploits never opens. Responsibilism's strength is its fidelity to actual epistemic life, where the salient failures — dogmatism, wishful thinking, negligence — are failures of character, not of eyesight.

The view pays a famous dividend on the value problem. If knowledge is true belief produced by a reliable process, it is unclear why it beats mere true belief — reliability adds nothing once the truth is in hand, as good espresso is no better for coming from a reliable machine. But if knowledge is an *achievement* — success creditable to the agent's own competence — it inherits the value achievements generally have over lucky successes, as a hole-in-one skillfully made differs from one shanked in off a tree.

Two developments now drive the field. The **situationist challenge**, imported from ethics, marshals empirical psychology to argue that broad, stable intellectual character traits are largely mythical: performance tracks trivial situational variables, so a theory of knowledge built on virtues rests on traits few possess. Defenders reply that competences are more local, or that responsibilist virtues are ideals to regulate inquiry, not descriptions of the average inquirer. Second, a flourishing literature simply *uses* the framework, profiling particular virtues and vices with a specificity older epistemology never attempted: intellectual humility, curiosity, and — in Cassam's obstructivist vice epistemology — closed-mindedness, arrogance, and gullibility, analyzed as culpable obstacles to inquiry with real-world casework from conspiracy theory to intelligence failure. Whether virtue epistemology is the correct analysis of knowledge or a fruitful change of subject is itself contested; the second claim now looks harder to deny than the first.

## Children

- `reliabilism` — **Virtue Reliabilism** — competences, the AAA structure, apt belief, and the animal/reflective distinction. *(standard)*
- `responsibilism` — **Virtue Responsibilism** — intellectual character traits, motivation by truth, and Zagzebski's virtue-theoretic analysis of knowledge. *(standard)*
- `vice` — **Vice Epistemology** — epistemic vices as culpable obstacles to knowledge: Cassam's obstructivism, closed-mindedness, arrogance. *(standard)*
- `situationism` — **The Situationist Challenge** — whether empirical psychology undermines the attribution of stable intellectual virtues. *(satellite)*

## Bridges

- To `ethics`: the whole apparatus — virtue, character, skill, flourishing — is borrowed from virtue ethics, and the situationist objection arrived through the same door.
- To `epistemology.knowledge`: knowledge-as-achievement is the virtue theorist's answer to Gettier and to the Meno value problem homed there.
- To `epistemology.justification`: virtue reliabilism is process reliabilism refined to answer internalist objections; the internalism–externalism divide runs between the program's two wings.
- To `epistemology.social`: vice epistemology and the study of intellectual humility supply the working vocabulary for expertise, testimony, and institutional dysfunction.
