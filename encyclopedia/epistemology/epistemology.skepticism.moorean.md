# Moorean Responses and Dogmatism

Moore held up his hands. Here is one hand, here is another; therefore two human hands exist; therefore an external world exists. He conceded he could not prove the premise and insisted the proof was rigorous anyway: the premise was more certain than any premise of any argument against it. That is the Moorean shift. A skeptical argument and its reversal share every premise but one; which is the reductio of the other is settled by comparative plausibility, and "I have hands" beats "if I do not know I am not envatted, I do not know I have hands." Its strength is that it answers with exactly what the conclusion denies; its weakness is that it says nothing about *why* the skeptic's premise, which looked compelling, is the one to discard. The contemporary debate is about supplying that.

**Transmission failure.** Wright's diagnosis is that Moore's proof is valid, its premises warranted, and yet the warrant does not transmit to the conclusion. Experience warrants "here is a hand" only for someone already entitled to take for granted an external world in which experience is a guide; so the conclusion cannot be first warranted *by* the argument. The template: inference fails to transmit warrant when warrant for the premise presupposes warrant for the conclusion. Wright's positive proposal is that the presupposition is not known but something we are *entitled* to — a rational, non-evidential standing to trust, since inquiry is impossible without trusting something and the trust cannot be earned inside the inquiry. Here the Moorean and hinge traditions meet; the entitlement is adjudicated at `hinge`. Critics (Pritchard, Jenkins) ask whether an entitlement grounded in our predicament rather than in evidence is epistemic at all, or merely pragmatic.

**Dogmatism.** Pryor's alternative denies the presupposition. Perceptual experience as of a hand confers immediate, prima facie justification for believing there is a hand — defeasible, but not dependent on antecedent justification for denying skeptical hypotheses. What earns this is the phenomenology of perception: experiences present their contents with an assertoric force that imagination lacks, and that force makes them justifiers. Moore's argument then transmits; the skeptic erred in thinking warrant for the premise must first exclude the alternatives. Dogmatism is the position best placed to make the Moorean shift principled, and it has drawn the most fire. White's Bayesian objection: a skeptical hypothesis predicts the very experience, so having it cannot raise the probability that one is not deceived, and may lower it. Cohen's easy knowledge and Vogel's bootstrapping: if experience justifies without background credentials, one can learn one's faculties are reliable from their own deliverances. And cognitive penetration (Siegel): if belief or fear can shape perceptual content, an experience produced by an unjustified belief and taken as immediate support for it is a circular check — and dogmatism must either deny that penetrated experiences justify, reintroducing background conditions, or accept the circle. Whether penetration occurs at all is contested in vision science; the case is adjudicated with `mind.perception`.

**Neo-Moorean externalism.** Sosa and Williamson keep Moore's confidence but relocate its ground. For Sosa, one knows one has hands because the belief is safe — could not easily have been false — and manifests a competence; the skeptical scenario, being distant, does not defeat safety, and reflective knowledge of this is available from within. For Williamson, knowledge is evidence: one's evidence that one has hands *is* that one has hands, a fact the envatted brain lacks, so the premise that the two subjects share evidence is false and skepticism loses its starting point. The objection to both is that they explain why the Moorean knows, not how the Moorean can *tell*; the skeptic's question returns as Stroud's demand for a standpoint from which one's own faculties are assessed. The neo-Moorean replies that no theory could meet that demand, and a response owes nothing to an ambition nobody can satisfy.
