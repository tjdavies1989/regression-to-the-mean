# Philosophy of Religion

Philosophy of religion asks whether there is a God, what such a being could be like, and what it would be reasonable to believe about either question. The division is hard because its central object, if real, is unlike anything else philosophy handles: not one more item in the world but the alleged reason there is a world at all — so that the philosopher's ordinary instruments, calibrated on ordinary things, are themselves on trial in every argument. The field's modern form is an analytic revival: after a positivist half-century in which its sentences were ruled meaningless, the past sixty years rebuilt natural theology, its refutations, and the epistemology of faith with full technical rigor — while a widening flank asks whether the whole framing, theism versus its denial, is a parochially Abrahamic way to carve religion at all.

**The arguments for God's existence** are philosophy's longest-running research program. The cosmological family — Kalām's beginning-of-the-universe version, Leibnizian arguments from contingency, Aquinas's ways, and Ibn Sīnā's proof of the Necessary Existent from the analysis of existence itself — trades on why there is something rather than nothing, against the brute-fact reply. The ontological family tries to close the question from the armchair, Anselm through the modal versions of Plantinga and Gödel, with Kant's existence-is-not-a-predicate as the standing objection each generation refits. The design family fell to Hume and Darwin and rose again as fine-tuning, where the multiverse and anthropic selection effects now carry the skeptical load. Swinburne's cumulative Bayesian case is the methodological frontier: no single proof, but a confirmation theory of theism.

**The problem of evil** is the engine running in reverse, and the division's most consequential debate. The logical version stands officially solved by Plantinga's free-will defense — a verdict lately reopened — leaving the evidential versions (Rowe's fawn, Draper's comparative hypotheses) to carry the attack: not contradiction but disconfirmation. Skeptical theism answers that we are in no position to survey God's reasons, at the acknowledged risk of a skepticism that spreads beyond theology; the theodicies — soul-making, free will, felix culpa — answer more ambitiously, against the anti-theodicy tradition that finds the genre itself indecent. Divine hiddenness (Schellenberg) — why would a loving God be resistible even to the willing? — has grown into evil's rival as the case for atheism.

**The divine attributes** house the coherence project: omnipotence and its stone, omniscience against creaturely freedom (Boethius to Molina to open theism), eternity as timelessness or everlastingness, simplicity's austere classical core — with Anselmian perfect-being methodology as the generator that proposes and tests each analysis. **Faith and reason** holds the epistemology: evidentialism's challenge, Reformed epistemology's properly-basic reply and the Great Pumpkin's revenge, religious experience as perception (Alston) or evidence (Swinburne), the pragmatic arguments from Pascal and James, the nature of faith itself as belief, trust, or acceptance — and now the debunking pressures from cognitive science of religion. **Religious language** keeps the old question of how talk of God could mean anything — analogy, apophaticism, non-cognitivism — at deliberate satellite depth. **Diversity** confronts the plurality of incompatible faiths: exclusivism through Hick's pluralism, and religious disagreement as peer disagreement's hardest case, since each tradition's experts are trained inside it. **Miracles** analyzes Hume's famous argument rather than reciting it; **the afterlife** asks what persons would have to be to survive death, with personal identity's constraints pressing on every answer and the Buddhist rebirth-without-self case treated on its own terms. **Non-theistic religious philosophy** widens the lens — emptiness, the Dao, nirguna Brahman, tian — and asks whether "religion" itself, a category with a colonial passport, distorts what it classifies.

The figures register anchors: `ibn-sina`, `aquinas` (stubbed), `al-ghazali` (stubbed), `hume`, and `kant` carry the classical debates; the traditions register carries what this division's theistic frame cannot.

## Bridges

- **Metaphysics**: necessary being, causation, time, and modality carry every classical argument; `metaphysics.modality`'s epistemology prices the ontological argument.
- **Epistemology**: faith and reason is applied epistemology under maximal stakes; religious disagreement stresses `epistemology.disagreement`.
- **Mind** and **metaphysics.personal-identity**: souls and survival.
- **Ethics**: divine command and the Euthyphro; the problem of evil is applied axiology.
- **Traditions**: `buddhist-philosophy`, `chinese-philosophy`, `indian-philosophy`, and `islamic-philosophy` hold the lives this division's arguments abstract from.
