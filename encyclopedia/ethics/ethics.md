# Ethics

Ethics asks what we owe one another, what is worth wanting, and what kind of person to be. Everyone already operates with answers, yet they collide when pressed. A runaway trolley will kill five unless diverted onto a track where it kills one; most would divert it. Push a heavy man off a footbridge to stop the same trolley, saving the same five, and most recoil. If only outcomes matter, the cases are identical. Consequentialists, for whom rightness just is producing the best outcome, must explain the recoil away or capture it indirectly through rules. Deontologists hold that the recoil tracks something real — persons may not be used merely as means, and some things may not be done even to prevent worse — but must say why a constraint binds when violating it would mean fewer violations overall. Virtue ethics, revived by Anscombe, changes the question: not "which acts are right?" but "what would the practically wise person do?", grounding right action in character and flourishing.

Beneath the rivalry sits a second-order question: is there anything here to be right *about*? Moral convictions vary across cultures yet feel, from inside, like perceptions of fact. Mackie's argument from queerness says they cannot be: objective values would be unlike anything else in the world, known by a faculty unlike any other. Realists reply that moral facts are no stranger than mathematical ones. Expressivists reinterpret the talk — moral claims express attitudes, not descriptions — then labor to explain why it behaves so like description. Error theorists grant the aim at fact and conclude the talk systematically misses. Constructivists build objectivity from what agents would agree to, or could not rationally reject. Behind all stands Glaucon's question: why be moral, when injustice pays and no one is watching?

A third strand concerns value itself: what makes a life go well — pleasure, getting what one wants, or objective goods that benefit a person whether or not she wants them? Nozick's experience machine is the test: if you would not plug into simulated bliss, pleasure is not all that matters.

The field articulates into three tiers — metaethics (the status and meaning of moral claims), normative theory (rival accounts of right action and good character), and applied ethics (practical fields, each with its own literature) — ringed by value theory, moral psychology, moral epistemology, and responsibility. Its boundaries: justice among institutions belongs to politics, free will to metaphysics (though responsibility is argued here), moral motivation faces mind, divine command faces religion.

## Children

- `metaethics` — **Metaethics** — whether moral claims state facts, what moral terms mean, and where moral authority could come from. *(anchor)*
- `value` — **Value Theory** — what is good in itself: well-being, pleasure, desire-satisfaction, and the plurality of intrinsic values. *(anchor)*
- `consequentialism` — **Consequentialism** — rightness as producing the best outcome, from classical utilitarianism to its indirect refinements. *(anchor)*
- `deontology` — **Deontology** — duties, rights, and constraints that bind whatever the consequences; Kant and his heirs. *(anchor)*
- `virtue-ethics` — **Virtue Ethics** — character, practical wisdom, and flourishing as the primary ethical categories. *(anchor)*
- `moral-psychology` — **Moral Psychology** — moral motivation, emotion, and judgment, and what their empirical study means for theory; bridges mind. *(standard)*
- `moral-epistemology` — **Moral Epistemology** — how moral claims are known or justified: intuitions, reflective equilibrium, and debunking arguments. *(standard)*
- `moral-responsibility` — **Moral Responsibility** — blame, praise, and desert, and what they require of agents; bridges free will, homed in metaphysics. *(anchor)*
- `applied-ethics` — **Applied Ethics** — the hub for practical fields with their own literatures: bioethics, AI ethics, environmental ethics. *(anchor)*
- `future-people` — **Future People and Population Ethics** — obligations to those who do not yet exist: the non-identity problem and the paradoxes of population axiology. *(standard)*

## Bridges

- To `metaphysics`: moral realism is an ontological thesis; free will is homed there, while responsibility lives here.
- To `mind`: moral psychology and action theory are shared territory; whether moral judgment necessarily motivates belongs to both.
- To `epistemology` and `language`: moral epistemology imports the theory of justification; expressivism is as much semantic thesis as moral one.
- To `politics`, `religion`, and the registers: justice extends these questions to institutions; the Euthyphro dilemma binds them to theology; the spine of figures runs from Aristotle and Mencius through Kant, Mill, and Parfit.
