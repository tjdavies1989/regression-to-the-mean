# The Analytic Tradition

The analytic tradition is the only philosophical movement to have become, in much of the world, simply "the profession" — which makes it the hardest to see as a tradition at all, with founding contingencies, a house style, and borders patrolled by feel rather than doctrine. It begins, on the standard telling, twice: with Frege's invention of quantificational logic and his insistence that thoughts be studied through language rather than psychology, and with Moore's and Russell's revolt against British absolute idealism around 1900 — the conviction that reality is many things, not one, and that philosophy's method is the analysis of propositions into their real constituents. Russell's theory of descriptions became the movement's "paradigm of philosophy": a showpiece of how logical form, hidden beneath grammatical form, dissolves metaphysical puzzles.

What followed is better described as a sequence of self-overthrows than a research program. The Vienna Circle radicalized analysis into logical empiricism: meaningfulness confined to the verifiable, metaphysics diagnosed as noise. Wittgenstein, claimed as inspiration by the Circle, repudiated the reading and then the picture itself, turning to language as motley practice; ordinary-language philosophy at Oxford made that turn institutional. Quine then demolished the analytic–synthetic distinction on which the whole program of "conceptual analysis" seemed to rest — from inside, using the tradition's own standards, which is the tradition's characteristic way of changing its mind. The result, from the 1970s, was the great unclenching: Kripke's necessary a posteriori and the revival of modal metaphysics, Rawls's revival of normative political theory, and the naturalistic turn that handed philosophy of mind to the cognitive sciences' border. Late analytic philosophy freely does what early analytic philosophy existed to forbid.

What, then, unifies it? Not doctrine — every founding thesis has been abandoned by analytic philosophers in good standing. The live candidates: a genealogy (the Frege–Russell–Vienna descent), a style (argument-forward, formalism-tolerant, piecemeal rather than systematic), and a set of institutional norms about what counts as rigor. Critics inside and out — often under the contested label "continental," which names the tradition's constructed other more than any real school — press that the style itself carries commitments: ahistoricism, false neutrality, a scientism that mistakes tidiness for truth. The tradition's own historians (the "history of analytic philosophy" is now a thriving subfield, which is itself a datum) increasingly agree that the self-image of pure method was always a myth, sustained by forgetting its idealist and Kantian inheritances.

## Bridges

The tradition's formative bridges run to `logic` (its birthplace and long-standing house organ) and `language` (its central division for half a century); to `figures/` it is the register's densest neighborhood — `frege`, `russell`, `moore`, `wittgenstein`, `carnap`, `quine`, `kripke`, `lewis` are all primarily read within it. Its self-understanding is a standing case study for `meta.what-is-philosophy`.
