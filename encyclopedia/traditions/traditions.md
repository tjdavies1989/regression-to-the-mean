# Traditions

A problems-first encyclopedia runs a known risk: it quietly universalizes the way one tradition — in our case, broadly the Western and latterly the analytic one — carves the problems. This register is the corrective. It treats periods, schools, regional traditions, and movements as living wholes: not as quarries for "contributions" to our problem tree, but as ways of organizing philosophical life that sometimes carve the terrain differently, and whose difference is itself philosophically instructive. Nyāya epistemology is not early Gettierology; Madhyamaka is not sloppy paraconsistency; reading them as such is exactly the flattening this register exists to resist. Where genuine convergences exist — and they do — the tradition article is where the comparison is made carefully.

"Tradition" here covers three kinds of thing, deliberately mixed: **periods** of a lineage (Hellenistic philosophy, early modern Europe), **regional traditions** with their own canons and institutions (Indian, Chinese, Islamic philosophy), and **movements** that cut across time and place (phenomenology, pragmatism, feminist philosophy). The coarse articles come first; each will descend into its own internal articulation by the ordinary pulse rules — `indian-philosophy` alone contains a dozen schools that will eventually demand their own layer.

The register's first two sketches are, deliberately, its two poles: `analytic`, the tradition that supplied the problem tree's own vocabulary and must therefore be seen *as* a tradition rather than as the neutral frame; and `indian-philosophy`, the largest standing demonstration that the terrain can be carved otherwise.

## Children

- `presocratics` — **The Presocratics** — the first physicists and the invention of argument, from Thales to the atomists. *(standard)*
- `classical-athens` — **Classical Athens** — Socrates, Plato, Aristotle, and the sophists: philosophy's founding institutions. *(anchor)*
- `hellenistic` — **Hellenistic Philosophy** — Stoics, Epicureans, and skeptics: philosophy as the art of living. *(anchor)*
- `neoplatonism` — **Neoplatonism** — Plotinus and the late-antique metaphysics of emanation. *(standard)*
- `indian-philosophy` — **Indian Philosophy** — the darśanas: Nyāya, Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta, Sāṃkhya, and their debates with Buddhist and Jain thought. *(anchor)*
- `buddhist-philosophy` — **Buddhist Philosophy** — no-self, dependent origination, emptiness, and a rigorous epistemological tradition, from India through Tibet and East Asia. *(anchor)*
- `chinese-philosophy` — **Chinese Philosophy** — Confucian, Daoist, Mohist, and Legalist thought and their long synthesis. *(anchor)*
- `islamic-philosophy` — **Islamic Philosophy** — falsafa and kalām: the Greek inheritance transformed, from al-Kindī to Mullā Ṣadrā. *(anchor)*
- `jewish-philosophy` — **Jewish Philosophy** — from Philo and Maimonides to Spinoza's heresy and twentieth-century thought. *(standard)*
- `medieval-latin` — **Medieval Latin Philosophy** — scholasticism: logic, metaphysics, and theology in the universities. *(anchor)*
- `renaissance` — **Renaissance Philosophy** — humanism, the revival of ancient schools, and the road to the new science. *(satellite)*
- `early-modern` — **Early Modern Philosophy** — from Descartes to Kant: the epistemological turn and the rationalist–empiricist contest. *(anchor)*
- `german-idealism` — **Kant and German Idealism** — the critical philosophy and its absolute successors. *(anchor)*
- `nineteenth-century` — **The Nineteenth Century** — Hegel's aftermath: historicism, materialism, existential revolt. *(standard)*
- `pragmatism` — **Pragmatism** — Peirce, James, Dewey: truth and meaning tied to practice. *(standard)*
- `phenomenology` — **Phenomenology** — Husserl's method and its transformations in Heidegger, Merleau-Ponty, Levinas. *(anchor)*
- `existentialism` — **Existentialism** — freedom, absurdity, and authenticity as philosophical themes. *(standard)*
- `analytic` — **The Analytic Tradition** — from Frege and Russell through logical empiricism to the present mainstream. *(anchor)*
- `critical-theory` — **Critical Theory** — the Frankfurt School and the critique of rationalized society. *(standard)*
- `feminist-philosophy` — **Feminist Philosophy** — gender as a lens on knowledge, ethics, politics, and the canon itself. *(standard)*
- `africana` — **Africana Philosophy** — African and diasporic thought: personhood, race, liberation. *(standard)*
- `latin-american` — **Latin American Philosophy** — identity, liberation philosophy, and the politics of philosophical dependence. *(standard)*
- `kyoto-school` — **The Kyoto School** — Nishida and successors: nothingness between Zen and German philosophy. *(satellite)*

## Bridges

The register's structural partner is `figures/` — every figure is read within a tradition, every tradition article names its load-bearing figures. Into the problem tree, the densest bridges currently run from `analytic` and `early-modern` (which shaped the tree's own vocabulary), from `indian-philosophy` and `buddhist-philosophy` into epistemology, mind, and metaphysics, and from `critical-theory` and `feminist-philosophy` into politics and epistemology.
