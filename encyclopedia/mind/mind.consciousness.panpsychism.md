# Panpsychism and Russellian Monism

Physics, Russell argued in *The Analysis of Matter* (1927) and Eddington the same year, says what matter does and how it is arranged — mass is what resists acceleration, charge what a field moves — and never what it is that does these things. Its vocabulary is exhausted by structure and dynamics; the intrinsic nature of whatever bears the structure is left blank. Russellian monism fills the blank with consciousness or its precursors: the categorical base of the physical world is (proto)phenomenal, and the hard problem arose because physicalism mistook a structural description for a complete inventory. It is monism because there is one kind of stuff, Russellian because that stuff is known to physics only by its relational profile.

The case for it is a squeeze between two failures. The anti-physicalist arguments show that structure and dynamics do not entail experience; dualism accepts this and pays with epiphenomenalism, since physics is causally closed. Russellian monism escapes both horns: experience is not added to the physical but is what the physical intrinsically is, so it inherits the causal roles physics describes. Chalmers accordingly treats it as the one exit from his zombie argument — if P is silent on intrinsic natures, a world can match ours in P and differ in what makes P true. Strawson's realistic monism (2006) supplies the second pressure: a physicalist who takes experience as real must either find it in the base or accept that it emerges at some threshold by brute law, and Strawson holds that emergence of the experiential from the wholly non-experiential is not brute but unintelligible. Goff (2017) adds parsimony: among views that honour both the datum and causal closure, panpsychism is the simplest.

The positions differ on what fills the blank and at which level. Constitutive panpsychism puts genuine micro-experience — micro-subjects with micro-qualities — in the fundamental entities, and holds that human experience is constituted by their arrangement. Panprotopsychism, Chalmers's preferred variant, posits protophenomenal properties that are not experiential but yield experience non-brutely when combined. Cosmopsychism (Goff, Shani, Nagasawa) reverses the direction of grounding: the fundamental subject is the cosmos and finite minds are derivative — priority monism carried into philosophy of mind. Emergentist panpsychism grants that macro-experience emerges from micro-experience by law, surrendering the anti-emergentist argument that motivated the view.

The standing objection is the combination problem, named by Seager (1995) out of James's complaint that a hundred feelings put together do not make a hundred-and-first. Chalmers (2016) separated three forms. The **subject-summing** problem is a conceivability argument turned on the panpsychist: it seems coherent that micro-subjects be arranged exactly as they are in a brain while no macro-subject exists, so micro-subjects do not necessitate one — a Russellian zombie, by the view's own standards. The **palette** problem is that a few fundamental kinds of quality, perhaps as few as the fundamental physical properties, must yield the full range of experienced qualities, and no one has said how a small palette blends into tastes, pains, and hues. The **structural mismatch** problem is that the structure physics attributes to a brain — discrete, spatial, particulate — is unlike the smooth, unified structure of a visual field, yet if micro-experience mirrors micro-physics its combination should mirror neural structure. Cosmopsychism trades these for a decombination problem: how one cosmic subject yields many bounded subjects that are neither identical with it nor mere parts of it. Replies include Goff's phenomenal bonding relation, Seager's fusion, and Roelofs's argument that subject-summing is no worse than material composition generally.

Where the view stands: respectable again after a century's neglect, with dedicated anthologies (Alter and Nagasawa 2015; Brüntrup and Jaskolla 2017) and Chalmers's judgement that it is the most promising option nobody has made work. Its costs are three. Chalmers concedes that the combination problem may be the hard problem relocated, so that the view explains nothing until it is solved. Howell argues that the intrinsic natures do no causal work — the structural roles do — so Russellian monism's advertised escape from epiphenomenalism fails. And the blank may be empty rather than unfilled: dispositional essentialism denies that properties have quiddities at all, and ontic structural realism denies there is anything beyond structure. Stoljar's dilemma cuts the other way: if the intrinsic base is physical, physicalism was never refuted, and the view is physicalism in letter and dualism in spirit — which is the parent node's standing charge.
