# Aesthetics

A term that covers Chartres cathedral, a Beethoven quartet, *Ulysses*, and a signed urinal — and, beyond the artworld, sunsets, gardens, and a well-laid table — names the division's terrain: the beautiful, the artful, and the strange kind of judgment we pass on them. Aesthetics is philosophy's laboratory for a distinctive puzzle: judgments that rest on nothing but felt response yet claim more than personal report — Kant's scandal of a pleasure that demands everyone's agreement. The modern field runs on two tracks that keep crossing: the theory of the aesthetic (experience, judgment, beauty), and the philosophy of art (what art is, what kind of thing a work is, what it means, and what it does to and with morality and emotion), extended through the individual art forms, each of which turns out to philosophize differently.

**The definition of art** is the field's oldest project and best graveyard: mimesis died of abstraction, expression died twice, formalism died of circularity and conceptual art — each theory canonizing one movement and falsified by the next, often deliberately. Weitz drew the anti-essentialist moral; Danto's indiscernibles (Brillo boxes perceptually identical to grocery cartons) relocated arthood from the eye to the artworld's atmosphere of theory; Dickie institutionalized the insight, Levinson historicized it, Gaut clustered it — and whether "art" needs defining or only understanding is now itself a position. **Aesthetic experience and judgment** holds the Kantian core — subjective universality, disinterest, free play — beside Hume's true judges and their circularity, Sibley's aesthetic concepts, the demolished attitude theories, and the testimony puzzle: why aesthetic deference seems hollow when scientific deference does not. **Beauty**, exiled by a century of art that refused it, returns as a research subject: response-dependence, the sublime as structured contrast, and the priority claims of natural beauty.

**The ontology of artworks** asks what kind of thing a work is — singular object or repeatable type — with creation, forgery (Goodman's perfect fake), performance, and restoration as the constraining data; **interpretation** contests whether the artist's intention fixes meaning, from the intentional fallacy through actual and hypothetical intentionalism to critical pluralism's question whether incompatible readings can share one work. **Art and emotion** owns the paradox industry: being moved by what we know is unreal, seeking out tragedy and horror, expression without a self expressed. **Art and morality** asks whether moral flaws are aesthetic ones — autonomism, moralism, ethicism, immoralism — with imaginative resistance underneath and the immoral-artist problem as its current public face.

**The art forms** each earn their philosophy: music, the hardest case — expressiveness, understanding, even profundity, in an art with no semantics; depiction, where pictures represent by means neither resemblance alone nor convention alone explains, with photography's claimed transparency as the live wire; literature, where the cognitivist question — can fiction teach, and how, without argument or evidence? — meets narrative and the heresy of paraphrase; film, a collaborative art whose authorship, realism, and spectatorship debates test every category built for older forms. **Environmental and everyday aesthetics** closes the circuit beyond the artworld — nature appreciated as what it is, the aesthetics of ordinary life, and the Japanese categories treated on their own terms — against the trivialization worry that an aesthetics of everything is an aesthetics of nothing.

The figures register anchors: `plato` and `aristotle` open the mimesis tradition, `hume` and `kant` the judgment tradition; `nietzsche` (stubbed) holds art's claim to rival philosophy itself. The division's densest borders: `mind` (emotion, perception, imagination), `language` (fiction, metaphor, interpretation), `ethics` (the art-morality debates).
