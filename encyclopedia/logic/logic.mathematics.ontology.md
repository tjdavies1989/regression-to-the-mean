# The Ontology of Mathematics

"There are infinitely many primes" has the grammar of an existence claim, the certainty of proof, and — if it is about anything — a subject matter no one has ever seen, touched, or caused to do anything. The ontology of mathematics asks whether to take the claim at face value: do mathematical objects exist, and if not, why is mathematics not simply false?

Platonism says face value is the only honest reading. Its strongest case descends from Frege. First, semantic uniformity: whatever semantics makes "there are eight planets" true in virtue of planets should make "there are two primes between ten and fifteen" true in virtue of primes. Numerals function as singular terms in sentences we hold true, and a semantics on which a true sentence's terms refer to nothing is a semantics invented for the occasion. Second, objectivity: theorems are found, forced on every mathematical culture, never voted in. Platonism explains both at a stroke: mathematics is the science of a mind-independent abstract domain. Its burden is the access problem. Benacerraf posed it as a dilemma between semantics and a causal epistemology; Field's sharpening survives the causal theory's demise. The platonist must explain the *reliability* of mathematicians' beliefs — the correlation between what they accept and what holds in a realm they cannot interact with — and no proposed mechanism has commanded agreement.

Nominalism refuses the objects and inherits the mirror-image debt: explaining why mathematics works. Field's fictionalism is the most disciplined attempt. Mathematical statements are untrue, as statements about Sherlock Holmes are untrue; mathematics is good without being true because it is *conservative* — adding it to a nominalistic science yields no conclusions about the concrete world not already derivable without it. Field's *Science Without Numbers* showed the program working for Newtonian gravitation, reformulated in terms of spacetime regions. Its Achilles' heel is the rest of physics: no one has nominalized quantum mechanics, whose Hilbert-space formalism has resisted every concrete surrogate. Paraphrase strategies take a different route, reading mathematics as disguised modal truth — Hellman's claims about possible structures, Chihara's about constructible tokens — at the price of a primitive modality arguably no better understood than abstracta. Colyvan's distinction now organizes the field: hard-road nominalists do Field's reconstructive work; easy-road nominalists (Melia's "weaseling," Yablo's figuralism, Azzouni's deflationary reading of the quantifier) concede that mathematics is indispensable and deny that quantifying over something commits you to it. Whether that denial can be made principled rather than convenient is the live question.

The argument nominalists must ultimately defeat is indispensability. Quine and Putnam argued: we ought to believe in all and only the entities indispensable to our best science; mathematical entities are indispensable; therefore we ought to believe in them. The enhanced version narrows the load-bearing premise to explanation: mathematics does not merely represent physical facts but explains them — the periodical cicada's prime-numbered life cycle minimizes predator overlap *because* primes minimize common multiples; the honeycomb's hexagons answer to a minimization theorem — and inference to the best explanation then commits us to what the explanation quantifies over. The dissents are sharp. Maddy: fidelity to scientific practice, the argument's own naturalist standard, cuts against it — scientists quantify over continuous fluids and frictionless planes without believing in them, so figuring in best theory is not commitment. Sober: since every competing hypothesis shares the same mathematics, no observation confirms it against a rival; mathematics is never tested, only presupposed.

The debate stands as a standoff between two unpaid explanatory debts — access for the platonist, applicability for the nominalist. Structuralism and neo-logicism, treated in sibling entries, are best read as attempts to shrink the first without incurring the second.

## Bridges

- **Metaphysics**: the general ontology of abstract objects is the wider arena; every position here instantiates a stance there.
- **Epistemology**: the access problem is a reliability challenge — the same skeptical form that arises for moral and modal knowledge.
- **Science**: indispensability stands or falls with confirmation holism and inference to the best explanation; Maddy's and Sober's dissents are philosophy of science deployed as ontology.
