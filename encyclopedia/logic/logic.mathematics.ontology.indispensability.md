# The Indispensability Argument

In Colyvan's now-canonical form the argument has two premises: we ought to be ontologically committed to all and only the entities indispensable to our best scientific theories; mathematical entities are so indispensable; therefore we ought to be committed to mathematical entities. The first premise is not primitive — it is the fusion of two Quinean doctrines, and every serious attack targets the seam. *Naturalism* supplies the "all and only": there is no first philosophy, so ontology is read off science rather than legislated to it. *Confirmational holism* supplies the transmission mechanism: evidence confirms a theory as a corporate body, so confirmation reaches the mathematical apparatus along with the quarks. Strip out holism and confirmation might distribute selectively, stopping short of the numbers; strip out naturalism and a philosopher could accept the science and veto its ontology. (The "Quine–Putnam" label is loose: Putnam's *Philosophy of Logic* deployed indispensability for the objectivity of mathematics, not for objects, and he later disowned the platonist reading.)

Maddy's attack turns naturalism against holism. Look at practice: scientists quantify over continuous fluids, infinitely deep oceans, frictionless planes, and point masses while regarding them as false idealizations; and the history of atomic theory shows the converse — atoms were indispensable to chemistry from the 1860s, yet chemists properly withheld belief until Perrin's Brownian-motion experiments of 1913. Indispensable occurrence in best theory is neither sufficient for scientific belief nor, apparently, what triggers it. So holism is false *as a description of confirmational practice*, and naturalism — the argument's own standard — instructs us to defer to practice. The premises conflict; the argument devours itself. Sober's attack is confirmational rather than practice-based. On his contrastive account, evidence supports a hypothesis only against rivals that assign the observations different likelihoods. Every live scientific rival shares the same mathematics, so no observation ever discriminates for it: mathematics is presupposed by testing, never itself tested, and inherits no empirical support from science's success. The standing worry for both attacks is overreach — Sober's insulation argument threatens to strand logic and methodology equally beyond confirmation's reach.

The explanatory turn was the platonist's reply. Baker's *enhanced* indispensability argument narrows the load-bearing role from occurrence in theory to occurrence in *explanation*: inference to the best explanation commits us to what our best explanations quantify over, and some explanations of physical facts are mathematical through and through. The periodical cicada's 13- and 17-year life cycles minimize coincidence with periodic predators because primes minimize common multiples; the honeycomb's hexagons answer to Hales's isoperimetric theorem; no Eulerian walk crosses Königsberg's bridges because the multigraph has four odd-degree vertices. This bypasses Maddy — explanation, unlike idealized representation, is exactly where scientists do invest belief — and answers Sober by locating mathematics' confirmational currency in explanatory power rather than differential prediction. The counterattack, pressed sharpest by Saatsi, is that the mathematics plays only a "thin," representational role: it indexes the physical facts (the cicadas' actual eclosion patterns) that do the explaining, and a commitment-free paraphrase loses nothing explanatory. Baker's rejoinder is that the modal generality of these explanations — *any* prime period, *any* walker on those bridges — is precisely what no catalogue of physical facts delivers. That dispute, over what makes an explanation genuinely mathematical, is where the argument now lives.

Field's program remains the other exit: deny the second premise outright by nominalizing physics, so that mathematics is dispensable and its utility explained by conservativeness. Its stall at quantum mechanics is why the argument still stands. It is the load-bearing wall of contemporary platonism — Colyvan calls it the only good argument for mathematical realism — and its fate is now entangled with scientific realism's: whoever settles what inference to the best explanation commits us to settles this too.

## Bridges

- **Science**: the argument is philosophy of science deployed as ontology — its premises are confirmation holism and inference to the best explanation, and its enhanced form rises or falls with the realism debate over IBE.
- **Metaphysics**: a sound indispensability argument is the main extant route to abstract objects; the general debate over ontological commitment (Quine's criterion and its deflationary rivals) frames premise one.
