# Nominalist Reconstructions

The hard road concedes the indispensability argument its factual premise and attacks it with engineering: show that our best science can be *rewritten* without mathematical vocabulary, and the indispensability claim is false, not merely resisted. Field's *Science Without Numbers* (1980) is the existence proof for one theory, and its machinery repays close inspection because everything since is a reaction to it.

The machinery has two parts. First, nominalize the physics. Field's ontology is substantival spacetime: points and regions, taken as concrete, over which he quantifies with a logic of mereology. In place of "the gravitational potential at x is 3.2" — a relation between a point and a number — he uses comparative predicates intrinsic to the regions themselves: betweenness, congruence, "y is a potential-midpoint between x and z." The model is Hilbert's synthetic axiomatization of Euclidean geometry, generalized through Krantz–Suppes measurement theory. Second, the representation theorem: any structure satisfying the synthetic axioms admits a homomorphism into ℝ⁴, unique up to admissible transformation. Numbers thus enter as *bookkeeping* for intrinsic physical relations, the way meters enter for lengths. What licenses the bookkeeping is conservativeness: since mathematics-plus-nominalistic-physics yields no nominalistic consequence not already derivable, the platonist apparatus is a safe shortcut through reasoning one could, in principle, do the long way. One may use what one disbelieves, provided it is conservative — and, Field argues, good mathematics would be conservative even if false, which is why its falsity never shows up in practice.

The program's internal wound is logical. Shapiro showed that deductive and semantic conservativeness come apart for the second-order resources Field's mereology needs: there are nominalistic truths (Gödel sentences of the nominalistic theory) provable with mathematics but not without it. Field's retreat — a primitive modal notion of logical consequence — already spends the currency the modal nominalists spend openly. The external wound is quantum mechanics, and the block is specific, not a mere absence of effort. Malament's diagnosis: Field's method requires theories whose basic quantities are intrinsic attributions to spacetime regions, but a quantum state lives in Hilbert space, not spacetime — it is a functional on an abstract space with no concrete relata to bear synthetic surrogate relations, and quantum probabilities quantify over *possible* measurement outcomes, which are nobody's concreta. Balaguer's gambit of nominalizing via propensities physically instantiated in systems remains a sketch, and critics read propensities so construed as numbers in disguise.

The modal strategies buy the reconstruction with intensional coin. Hellman's modal structuralism translates arithmetic as: possibly there is an ω-sequence, and necessarily, whatever satisfies the Peano axioms satisfies the theorem — mathematics as the theory of possible structures, none actual. Chihara replaces existential quantification over sets with constructibility quantifiers: it is possible to construct an open-sentence token satisfying such-and-such. Both eliminate abstract objects; both take modality as primitive and unreduced. The standing charge — pressed by Field from one side and Burgess and Rosen from the other — is that this is abstracta by other means: the epistemology of unrealized possibilia is no more tractable than access to a platonic realm, and possible-structure talk without possible worlds is a promissory note.

There remains the bookkeeping question. Burgess and Rosen's dilemma: a reconstruction offered as what science *meant all along* is bad linguistics; offered as a *revolutionary* replacement, it should be submitted to physics journals, where its extra complexity would be rejected. Field's reply is that neither is the point — the reconstruction's mere existence defeats indispensability, whether or not anyone adopts it. Whether an unadopted paraphrase can carry that ontological weight is the debate's live nerve.

- **Science**: whether quantum mechanics admits any nominalistic formulation turns on its interpretation — a Hilbert-space realist and a Bohmian face different reconstruction problems.
