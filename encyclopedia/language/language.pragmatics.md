# Pragmatics and Speech Acts

"I now pronounce you married." The sentence describes nothing and reports nothing; uttered by the right person in the right circumstances, it *marries people*. Austin began with such performatives as a special class opposed to fact-stating constatives, then watched the distinction collapse under his own analysis: stating is as much a doing as promising is. What survived is an anatomy of every utterance. There is the locutionary act (producing a sentence with a certain meaning), the illocutionary act (what one does *in* uttering it — asserting, warning, christening, promising), and the perlocutionary act (what one does *by* uttering it — persuading, frightening, amusing). Illocutionary force became the field's central object: conventional, governed by felicity conditions, capable of misfiring rather than being false. Searle systematized the picture — speech acts as moves constituted by rules, as chess moves are, with a taxonomy (assertives, directives, commissives, expressives, declarations) and an analysis of how "Can you pass the salt?" performs one act indirectly by way of another.

Grice supplied the second engine. Say "Some students passed" and you convey that not all did — yet that is no part of what the sentence means, since it can be cancelled without contradiction ("indeed, all did"). Grice's diagnosis: conversation is a cooperative enterprise governed by maxims — be truthful, be adequately informative, be relevant, be perspicuous — and hearers interpret speakers as conforming to them, deriving *conversational implicatures* by working out what the speaker must believe for her utterance to be cooperative. The machinery is powerful because it is cheap: a lean semantics plus general rationality explains a vast range of conveyed meaning, and the derivations are calculable rather than stipulated. Its strength is precisely this economy — Occam's razor applied to senses, ambiguities dissolved into pragmatic effects.

But the machinery raised a boundary dispute that now organizes the field: where does semantics stop and pragmatics begin? Literalists and minimalists (Borg, Cappelen and Lepore) hold that sentences express complete, truth-evaluable propositions with only minimal contextual intrusion, pragmatics operating on top; their strength is preserving a systematic, compositional semantics. Contextualists (Travis, Recanati) argue that pragmatic processes penetrate what is said itself — "The leaves are green" shifts truth conditions with the interests in play — so that a context-free proposition is a theorist's fiction; their strength is fidelity to the shifting judgments of actual speakers. Relevance theory (Sperber and Wilson) replaces the maxims with a single cognitive principle — utterances raise expectations of relevance proportionate to processing effort — and treats interpretation as inference all the way down. The live question underneath: how much of intuitive "meaning" is pragmatic residue, and whether semantics shrinks to a skeleton that no utterance ever wears bare.

A further stratum concerns what conversations presuppose. "The king of France is bald" does not assert that France has a king; it takes it for granted, and the presupposition survives negation. Stalnaker modeled conversation as operating on a common ground of shared assumptions; Lewis generalized this into a conversational scoreboard tracking presuppositions, salience, and standards of precision — with the striking rule of *accommodation*: say something requiring a presupposition not yet on the board, and the board quietly updates to make your move count. Speech act theory has also turned political: Langton and others analyze silencing and subordination as illocutionary phenomena, asking what social conditions give words their force — and what deprives speakers of it.

## Bridges

- **language.meaning**: Grice's intention-based semantics is homed there; implicature is the same machinery run above the semantic floor, and the boundary wars decide how much that floor carries.
- **language.context**: contextualism's claim that pragmatics penetrates truth conditions is fought on indexicality's home ground — Kaplan's machinery is the minimalist's chief exhibit.
- **mind**: illocutionary acts are actions individuated by intention and uptake; pragmatic interpretation is mindreading, linking the field to intention and action theory.
- **politics**: silencing, propaganda, and subordinating speech import illocutionary analysis into social philosophy and the free-speech debates.
