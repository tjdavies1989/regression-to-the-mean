# Philosophy of Language

Physical marks and noises mean things: that is the standing miracle this division studies. A speaker who has never heard "the cat is on the mat" knows at once what it says, what would make it true, and what uttering it would do in a conversation — and a theory of language owes an account of all three competences. For half a century after Frege the field was not one division among others but analytic philosophy's method: if philosophical problems are knots in language, the theory of language is the theory of everything. That imperial phase ended — formal semantics emigrated to linguistics and became a cumulative science, the naturalists relocated meaning's foundations to the mind — but the foundational questions stayed home, and this division holds them.

**Meaning.** The rival programs each take one constraint as the master key. The truth-conditional program (Davidson's Tarski-style theories confirmed by radical interpretation; Montague's model-theoretic execution) takes the link to truth as constitutive and explains compositionality — how finite minds command infinite language — at the price of coarseness (co-extensive predicates, hyperintensional distinctions) and silence about what grasping a truth condition consists in. Use theories, from the later Wittgenstein's slogan to Brandom's inferentialism, make meaning a role in the practice of giving and asking for reasons — strongest exactly where the truth-conditional program is silent, laboring to recover compositionality and objectivity. Grice builds meaning from communicative intentions, semantics out of psychology; verificationism stands as the field's cautionary tale, a sharp criterion that could not state itself. Across all of them runs the internalist–externalist dispute — Chomsky's I-language against the Putnam–Burge argument that meanings are fixed by environment and community, not by what is in the head — and the unsettled question whether the programs compete or answer three different questions.

**Reference** is the field's showcase of philosophical progress. Frege's sense/reference distinction and Russell's theory of descriptions defined the descriptivist inheritance; Kripke's three arguments — modal, epistemic, semantic — broke it, and the causal-historical picture with rigid designation replaced it, carrying metaphysical cargo (the necessary a posteriori) that reshaped modality. Putnam's Twin Earth extended the externalist lesson to natural-kind terms. Empty names and negative existentials remain the recurring embarrassment of every theory; two-dimensionalism is descriptivism's disciplined revenge; and Lewisian reference magnetism — naturalness as a metasemantic tiebreaker — bridges the theory of reference to the metaphysics of properties.

**Truth** is homed here in its philosophical nature, with the paradoxes and formal theories split off to `logic.paradox` — a border whose seam is the conservativeness debate, where deflationism's fate hangs on formal results. The map: correspondence and its truthmaker descendants (converting the theory of truth into ontology); coherence and pragmatist theories binding truth to inquiry; deflationism as the modern default — truth as a device of generalization exhausted by the trivial biconditionals; pluralism (one truth role, many realizers) and primitivism (truth as bedrock) as the escapes from one-size-fits-all.

**The pragmatic wing.** Austin's discovery that speakers *do* things with words — the illocutionary anatomy Searle systematized — and Grice's conversational implicature opened the semantics/pragmatics border wars: literalism against contextualism against relevance theory on how much of intuitive meaning is pragmatic residue. Kaplan's character/content framework made indexicality tractable and exportable; the ensuing question is how far context-sensitivity spreads — quantifier domains, gradable adjectives, knowledge attributions — and whether some discourse (future contingents, taste, epistemic modals) demands MacFarlane's assessment-relativism rather than mere context-dependence.

**Attitude reports** put Frege's puzzle inside "believes that": substitution of co-referring names fails where reference alone says it should not, and Fregean, Russellian, and hidden-indexical semantics compete over Kripke's Pierre, the case that embarrasses everyone. The de se — thought about oneself as oneself (Lewis, Perry) — adds a dimension reference cannot capture, disputed lately as to whether it is philosophically special at all.

**Rule-following and normativity** is where the field questions its own foundations: Wittgenstein's paradox and Kripke's skeptic — no fact about a speaker constitutes meaning plus rather than quus — threaten meaning's determinacy itself; dispositionalist answers stumble on finitude and error, the skeptical solution goes communal, McDowell counsels quietism, and the debate over whether meaning is essentially normative (Boghossian against Hattiangadi) carries the problem into every naturalized theory of content. **Figurative language** — metaphor after Davidson's provocation that there is no metaphorical meaning, fiction after Walton's pretense theory and Lewis's truth-in-fiction — sits deliberately at satellite depth: real problems, thinner articulation.

The division's figures register is its founding roster — `frege`, `russell`, `wittgenstein`, `quine`, `kripke` — and its densest border runs to `mind`: intentionality inherits every problem meaning has, and whether language or thought is prior is itself the open question.

## Children

- `meaning` — **Theories of Meaning** — What meaning is and what a theory of it must deliver: truth-conditional, use-based, inferentialist, and intention-based programs. *(anchor)*
- `reference` — **Reference** — How words attach to things: sense and reference, descriptivism versus the causal-historical picture, rigid designation, and empty names. *(anchor)*
- `truth` — **Truth** — Whether truth is a substantive property or a deflationary device: correspondence, coherence, pragmatist, deflationary, pluralist, and primitivist accounts. *(anchor)*
- `pragmatics` — **Pragmatics and Speech Acts** — What speakers do with words: Austin and Searle on speech acts, Grice on implicature, and the semantics–pragmatics boundary. *(standard)*
- `attitudes` — **Propositional Attitude Reports** — The semantics of "believes that" and its kin, where substitution of co-referring terms breaks down; the de se. *(standard)*
- `context` — **Context-Sensitivity and Indexicality** — Kaplan's character–content framework, the spread of context-dependence, and assessment-relativism. *(standard)*
- `figurative` — **Figurative Language** — Metaphor and fictional discourse: meaning beyond the literal. *(satellite)*
- `rule-following` — **Rule-Following and Normativity** — Wittgenstein's paradox and Kripke's skeptic: what makes meaning determinate, and whether meaning is normative. *(standard)*

## Bridges

- **Logic**: formal semantics is joint property; truth's nature is homed here while its paradoxes live at `logic.paradox` — the conservativeness debate is the seam; Frege founded both fields in one stroke.
- **Mind**: intentionality and mental content inherit meaning's problems wholesale (`mind.intentionality`); attitude reports face both ways; whether thought or language is prior remains open.
- **Metaphysics**: rigid designation and the necessary a posteriori show semantics settling modal questions (`metaphysics.modality`); truthmaking hands truth's deepest questions to `metaphysics.grounding`; reference magnetism leans on `metaphysics.properties`.
- **Epistemology**: epistemic contextualism applies this division's machinery to "knows"; testimony transmits meaning as well as belief.
